# Miguel’s Talk Track (4–5 minutes)

> Presenter: **Miguel**, from the Philippines
>
> Tone: calm → personal → hopeful → energizing
>
> Notes: This is designed to feel like a short TED-style talk. It’s **emotional but not manipulative**—no guilt, no shaming—just honest stakes and a clear “why”.

---

## Opening (0:00–0:30)

Hi. My name is Miguel.

I grew up in the Philippines—where water wasn’t just something you turned on.
It was a season.
It was a sound.
It was a force.

When I was younger, I remember floods that made streets disappear.
Water flowed everywhere—through homes, through markets, through days that were supposed to feel normal.

Back then, water wasn’t a luxury.
It was a nuisance. Sometimes a danger.
And it shaped how you lived.

## The shift (0:30–1:30)

Now I live somewhere much drier.
And what’s strange is… the story flips.

Because here, water can feel like it’s always one step away from becoming a problem—
not because there’s too much,
but because there isn’t enough.

Clean water starts to feel like something you *hope* is available,
something you *assume* is safe,
something you might even have to *schedule*.

And the infrastructure—sometimes it’s laughably bad.
You end up relying on delivery trucks.
You start thinking about the simplest things:

- “Did we order enough?”
- “Will it arrive on time?”
- “Is this normal?”

That contrast is what hit me hardest.
I came from a place where water was everywhere.
To a place where clean water can feel like a luxury.

## Why this project exists (1:30–2:40)

So this project starts with a simple thought:

**What if we treated water like it matters—before it becomes an emergency?**

Because the truth is: we usually focus on the big systems.
The pipes.
The plants.
The policy.

But there’s a missing link we don’t talk about enough.

**Us.**
The end consumers.
The people who turn taps.
The people who water gardens.
The people who leave sprinklers on “just in case.”
The people who don’t get feedback until the bill arrives.

If people are the last mile… then people can also be part of the solution.
Not with lectures.
Not with guilt.

With clarity.
With feedback.
With a system that makes good choices easier.

## What you’re seeing (2:40–3:50)

That’s what this demo is.

It’s a self-presenting, interactive prototype that blends three ideas:

1. **Live telemetry** — a simulated solar-powered watering system that behaves like a real device.
2. **AI-assisted watering decisions** — it doesn’t just turn water on; it weighs conditions like moisture, tank levels, and energy.
3. **Gamification** — not to turn water into a game… but to make the invisible visible.

Because most of the time, waste happens quietly.
A few extra minutes.
A leak you don’t notice.
Habits that add up.

So we built a loop where actions create consequences you can see:

- You earn **Water Credits** by completing small habits.
- You spend credits when you choose to irrigate aggressively.
- You get nudged toward smarter decisions—not forced.

Even the mini-game is intentional.
It’s a metaphor:
water doesn’t reach the plant unless the path is connected.
And in real life, that “path” includes technology… and human behavior.

## The emotional core (3:50–4:30)

For me, this is personal.
Because I’ve lived both sides of water.

I’ve seen it overwhelm communities.
I’ve also seen it become something you ration.

And it taught me this:

**Water isn’t just a resource. It’s stability.**
It’s health.
It’s dignity.
It’s the difference between thriving and just coping.

## Close (4:30–5:00)

So when you click around this demo—
when you see the system adapt,
when you see the “AI” weigh tradeoffs,
when you see habits turn into outcomes—

I want you to remember one thing:

We don’t have to wait for a crisis to act like water matters.

If we build systems that respect water,
and help people make better choices,
then we’re not just saving a few liters.

We’re protecting the everyday life that water makes possible.

Thank you.
